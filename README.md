# Analysis of the Indian Rupee Exchange Rate

A re-analysis of an earlier college project on the monthly INR/USD exchange
rate. The original version regressed the exchange rate level on the CPI level
and an HP-filtered IIP growth cycle, and reported a very strong fit. Both
series are I(1), so that fit was largely spurious. This version tests
stationarity and cointegration first, then models monthly depreciation on
stationary variables.

## Data

Monthly data for India, January 2014 to July 2024 (127 observations).

| Variable | Description | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| monthly_exc_rate | Monthly average INR per USD | RBI DBIE |
| CPI | All-India CPI index (2012 = 100) | NSO, MoSPI |
| Actual_IIP | Year-on-year IIP growth, percent | NSO, MoSPI |

Two things to note about the variables:

- `Actual_IIP` is year-on-year growth, not month-on-month growth. April 2020
  is -57.3 percent relative to April 2019.
- The HP-filtered cycle of this series is a growth cycle, not the output gap.

## Method

1. ADF and KPSS tests for each series, plus bootstrap ADF tests for the two
   level series.
2. The original levels regression, with Durbin-Watson and Newey-West standard
   errors, to show what goes wrong.
3. An ARDL bounds test for cointegration, with exact p-values and two lag
   selection rules.
4. A corrected regression on stationary variables: monthly depreciation on
   monthly inflation and the IIP growth cycle, with Newey-West errors.

## Stationarity

| Series | ADF stat | 5% crit | KPSS stat | 5% crit | Read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exchange rate (level) | -0.66 | -2.88 | 2.43 | 0.46 | I(1) |
| CPI (level) | 2.38 | -2.88 | 2.59 | 0.46 | I(1) |
| IIP growth, YoY | -4.66 | -2.88 | 0.12 | 0.46 | I(0) |
| Exchange rate (d.log) | -7.66 | -2.88 | 0.05 | 0.46 | I(0) |
| CPI (d.log, inflation) | -7.34 | -2.88 | 0.07 | 0.46 | I(0) |

For the two level series I also ran bootstrap ADF tests (999 replications):
exchange rate p = 0.96 and CPI p = 0.85, so there is no evidence against a
unit root. The two level series are the ones that matter for the spurious
regression story.

## The original regression

`monthly_exc_rate ~ CPI + iip_cycle`, estimated by OLS with Newey-West
standard errors:

| | Estimate | HAC std. error | p-value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (Intercept) | 25.60 | 1.99 | < 2e-16 |
| CPI | 0.309 | 0.012 | < 2e-16 |
| IIP cycle | -0.081 | 0.013 | 5.1e-09 |

R-squared = 0.945, Durbin-Watson = 0.30.

The fit looks excellent and the coefficients look precise, but a Durbin-Watson
of 0.30 means the residuals are almost a random walk. Combined with the unit
root tests above, this is the textbook sign of a spurious levels regression.
Newey-West errors do not fix that; they only adjust the standard errors.

## Cointegration

ARDL bounds test on `monthly_exc_rate ~ CPI + iip_cycle`, case 3, with exact
p-values:

| Lag selection | Best order | Bounds F | Bounds t |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AIC | (5, 0, 1) | p = 0.043 | p = 0.021 |
| BIC | (1, 0, 1) | p = 0.166 | p = 0.105 |

The AIC-selected model suggests some evidence of a long-run relationship and
the BIC-selected model does not, so I do not treat cointegration as
established. It also depends on five lags of the dependent variable, which is
a lot for this sample.

Inflation has no association with monthly depreciation in any specification.
The IIP cycle coefficient is negative and around -0.02, but weak.

### Does the cycle result depend on the HP filter?

| Cycle | Estimate | HAC std. error | p-value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HP, lambda = 1600 | -0.0236 | 0.0079 | 0.003 |
| HP, lambda = 14400 | -0.0193 | 0.0102 | 0.061 |
| HP, lambda = 129600 | -0.0175 | 0.0103 | 0.092 |

The conventional monthly choices (14400 and 129600) give p-values of 0.06 and
0.09. The smaller lambda = 1600, which is the standard choice for annual data,
gives p = 0.003. Since the result moves with an arbitrary tuning parameter, I
would not report the cycle as a solid finding.

## What I take from this

- The original results were an artifact of regressing non-stationary levels on
  each other. The correct response is to test stationarity first, not to
  attach HAC errors to a levels regression and move on.
- Once the series are in first differences, there is no reliable short-run
  relationship between monthly depreciation and inflation in this sample.
- The negative association with the IIP growth cycle is suggestive at best and
  sensitive to the filter choice.
- The bounds test is a useful check, but the result should be reported with
  its lag-selection sensitivity rather than as a yes/no answer.

## How to run

You need R and the following packages:

```r
install.packages(c("readr", "lubridate", "dplyr", "ggplot2",
                   "urca", "bootUR", "ARDL", "mFilter",
                   "lmtest", "sandwich", "modelsummary", "sessioninfo"))
```

Then:

```r
source("analysis.R")
```

The bootstrap and the exact bounds test use a fixed seed, so the numbers are
reproducible. The script writes `session_info.txt` on each run.
